# Reviews — Honest Request Workflow and Response Templates

You currently have no public reviews. For a contractor, reviews are the strongest single
lever in local search and in the decision a customer makes before calling.

---

## The rules, stated plainly

Google's review policies prohibit incentivised and solicited-selectively reviews, and the
UAE has consumer-protection rules on misleading commercial practices. Beyond compliance,
these are simply the right way to work:

- ❌ **Never offer anything** for a review — no discount, no gift, no entry into a draw
- ❌ **Never review-gate** — don't ask "happy or unhappy?" and route only happy customers
  to Google. It's prohibited and it's dishonest
- ❌ **Never write or buy reviews**, or ask staff, family or friends to post as customers
- ❌ **Never ask a customer to remove** a negative review as a condition of anything
- ✅ **Ask every customer**, the same way, whether the job went brilliantly or not
- ✅ **Make it easy** — one link, one tap

A steady trickle of genuine reviews, including the occasional four-star, is more
convincing to a buyer than a wall of flawless five-star ones. People distrust perfection.

---

## When to ask

**At handover, in person, while the work is in front of them.** That's the moment
satisfaction is highest and the job is fresh. Then one WhatsApp follow-up with the link.

**One reminder only.** If they don't respond to that, stop. Chasing reviews annoys people
who might otherwise recommend you.

## The ask, out loud

> "If you're happy with how it turned out, a short Google review would really help us —
> most of our work comes from people finding us online. And if anything isn't right,
> tell me first and we'll sort it."

That last sentence is not gating. You're offering to fix problems, not filtering who
reviews you. The customer decides either way.

## The WhatsApp follow-up

```
Hello [Name], thank you again for choosing Burj Alfalah for your [kitchen/bathroom/
office] project.

If you're happy with the work, would you mind leaving a short Google review? It takes
a minute and genuinely helps people find us:

[REVIEW LINK]

And if anything needs attention, just message me here — we'd rather know.

[Your name], Burj Alfalah
```

**Getting your review link:** in your Google Business Profile, use "Ask for reviews" —
it generates a short link straight to the review box. Not available until the profile
exists and is verified.

## Where else to ask

Google first — it matters most. Once you have a steady flow there, Facebook reviews are
worth having. Don't spread thin; ten Google reviews beat three each across four platforms.

## Responding — templates to adapt, not paste

Reply to every review, good and bad. Responses are public and are often read more
carefully than the review itself. Future customers are judging **how you handle
problems**, not whether you ever have them.

### Positive, detailed
```
Thank you, [Name] — glad the [wardrobes] worked out well. The [oak finish] was a nice
one to make. Enjoy the new space, and do get in touch if you need anything else.
```
Mention the actual work. Generic "Thanks for the 5 stars!" replies read as automated.

### Positive, no detail (just stars)
```
Thank you, [Name] — much appreciated.
```
Short is right here. Don't pad it.

### Critical — the important one
```
Thank you for telling us, [Name], and I'm sorry the [delay on the second fix] affected
your project. You're right that we should have kept you better updated on the timeline.

I'd like to put it right. I'm on +971 56 328 7272 — please call me directly, or I can
call you at a time that suits.

[Your name], Burj Alfalah
```

**What makes this work:**
- Thanks them, doesn't get defensive
- Names the specific problem — shows you read it
- Accepts the part that's fair, without grovelling or admitting things that aren't true
- Moves to a phone call — public threads never resolve well
- Signed by a person

**Avoid:** arguing, contradicting their account, "we have no record of you as a
customer" (even if true, it reads badly), or copy-pasted corporate apology language.

### Unfair or fake review
Don't fight it publicly. Reply once, factually and calmly:
```
Thank you for the feedback. We don't have a record of a project matching this
description, so there may be a mix-up with another company. If you have worked with us,
please call +971 56 328 7272 and we'll look into it properly.
```
Then report it through the profile. Google's removal process is slow and often
unsuccessful — a calm public reply usually does more good than the report.

**Never** reply to a negative review while angry. Draft it, leave it an hour, then send.

---

## Tracking

Keep it simple — a sheet with: date, customer, project, platform, rating, whether you
responded, and anything to fix. Its real value isn't the count. It's spotting that three
separate people mentioned the same thing, which tells you something worth changing.

**Realistic pace:** 1–2 genuine reviews a month is healthy and sustainable. Twenty
appearing in a week looks bought, to customers and to Google alike.
